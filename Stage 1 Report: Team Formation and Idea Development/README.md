## 2. Idea Generation Process

### Brainstorming Approach

The team conducted collaborative brainstorming sessions in which each member proposed potential project ideas based on real-world problems, personal observations, and areas of interest.

The proposed ideas were discussed as a group to understand the problem each idea addressed, its target users, potential features, technical requirements, and whether a functional MVP could realistically be developed within the available timeframe.

Initial research was conducted to identify similar existing solutions and determine the potential value and differentiation of each concept. Based on these discussions and research, the team shortlisted the most promising ideas for further evaluation.

## 3. Ideas Explored & Rejection Rationale

### Idea 1: Dual-Care and Coordination Management Website for Parents of Children with Special Needs (Autism and Chronic Conditions)

**Strengths:**  
Highly targeted niche addressing fragmented care management, seamless parental collaboration through a shared daily log, and an emergency panic button displaying vital calming details instantly.

**Weaknesses:**  
User fatigue and burnout could hinder consistent daily data entry, and managing multiple complex medical datasets requires an ultra-simple interface to avoid adding extra stress.

**Reason for Rejection:**  
The core concept is already repetitive and saturated in the market compared to existing health and caregiver tracking platforms, lacking a truly disruptive unique selling proposition beyond standard features.

### Idea 2: Used Games Exchange Platform

**Strengths:**  
Provides a dedicated platform for gamers to buy, sell, and exchange used games and gaming accessories. Helps users save money by giving them access to cheaper second-hand games. Makes it easier for gamers to find specific games and connect with other gamers. Encourages reusing games instead of leaving them unused.

**Weaknesses:**  
Requires a large number of active users to provide enough games and offers. Trust and safety can be an issue when buying used products from individuals. Users may face problems with the condition or compatibility of used games and devices. Competing with established marketplaces would make it difficult to attract users.

**Reason for Rejection:**  
The concept is already available through established platforms such as Haraj and other classified marketplaces, where users can already sell and buy used games, consoles, and accessories. Therefore, the idea did not provide enough differentiation or a strong unique value proposition compared with existing platforms.

## 4. Evaluation and Selection of Ideas

### Evaluation Criteria

The team evaluated the proposed ideas based on the following criteria:

- **Problem Relevance:** Whether the idea addresses a clear and meaningful real-world problem.
- **Technical Feasibility:** Whether the team can implement the required technical components using its current skills and available resources.
- **MVP Feasibility:** Whether the core functionality can be completed within the available project timeframe.
- **Team Skills Alignment:** Whether the project aligns with the team's skills in frontend development, backend development, UI/UX, API integration, and GIS.
- **Innovation and Differentiation:** Whether the idea provides meaningful value or differentiation compared with existing solutions.
- **Scalability:** Whether the project can support additional features and future development beyond the initial MVP.

### Selection Process

The team compared the proposed ideas using the defined evaluation criteria and discussed their strengths, weaknesses, technical requirements, existing alternatives, and potential for future development.

The first two ideas addressed identifiable user needs but presented challenges related to differentiation and competition with existing solutions. After evaluating the alternatives, the team selected MAMAR because it addresses a specific real-world coordination problem, is technically feasible within the project timeframe, aligns with the team's technical skills, and provides opportunities for future development.

## 5. Selected MVP Concept

### Selected Idea Summary

MAMAR is a GIS-based B2B platform that connects contractors with traffic control service providers to manage temporary traffic control operations for construction and roadwork projects. Contractors can define their work zone on an interactive map, request traffic control services, and coordinate the implementation of temporary traffic elements such as road diversions, barriers, and traffic signs. Service providers can manage requests and document the actual field implementation using geographic locations and site information.

### The Problem It Solves

Construction and roadwork projects often require temporary traffic control measures, such as road diversions, barriers, traffic signs, and temporary traffic routes around work zones. Coordinating these services between contractors and traffic control service providers can involve multiple parties and requires clear location-based information.

MAMAR addresses this problem by providing a centralized GIS-based platform where contractors can define their work zone, request the required traffic control services, connect with suitable service providers, and document the implementation geographically.

### Target Audience

- **Contractors:** Construction, infrastructure, and roadwork contractors who require temporary traffic control services around their project work zones.
- **Traffic Control Service Providers:** Companies that provide and implement temporary traffic control services, such as road diversions, barriers, traffic signs, and temporary traffic routes.

### Key Features

**1. Interactive Work Zone Map:**  
Contractors can mark the exact project area and visualize the work zone on an interactive map.

**2. Service Request & Provider Selection:**  
Contractors can specify the required traffic control services and select an available service provider for the project.

**3. Project Status & Field Documentation:**  
Both parties can follow the request status, while service providers record completed field work and update project information.

### Rationale & Alignment

**Feasibility:**  
The project is achievable within the available timeframe using our team's technical skills in frontend and backend development, database management, API integration, and GIS. The MVP has a well-defined scope that allows the team to build and integrate the core system components progressively.

**Innovation & Impact:**  
MAMAR brings contractors and traffic control service providers into one digital workflow, simplifying service coordination and improving visibility throughout the process. The platform combines service management with location-based capabilities to provide a more organized and practical approach to temporary traffic control operations.

**Scalability:**  
The system is designed to support future growth by allowing new services and features to be added without changing the core workflow. Future versions could include real-time tracking, automated notifications, payment integration, advanced project dashboards, and additional traffic management services.

### Potential Challenges & Opportunities

**Challenges:**  
Managing development time effectively between software development tasks and preparing the required GIS layers and spatial data.

**Opportunities:**  
MAMAR has the potential to become a real market-ready product. As it supports existing governmental requirements related to temporary traffic control and work-zone management, it could create future opportunities for adoption by contractors and traffic control service providers, partnerships with relevant organizations, and expansion across infrastructure projects in Saudi Arabia.

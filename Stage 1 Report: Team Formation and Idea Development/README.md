2. Idea Generation Process
Brainstorming Approach

The team conducted collaborative brainstorming sessions in which members proposed project ideas based on real-world problems, personal observations, and areas that could benefit from a digital solution.

Each proposed idea was discussed as a group to understand the problem it addressed, its target users, possible features, technical requirements, and whether a functional MVP could realistically be developed within the available timeframe.

Initial research was also conducted to identify similar existing solutions and determine whether each idea provided enough value and differentiation. Based on these discussions and research, the team shortlisted the most promising ideas for further evaluation.

3. Ideas Explored & Rejection Rationale
Idea 1: Dual-Care and Coordination Management Website for Parents of Children with Special Needs

Strengths:
The idea targets a specific need related to fragmented care management and could support parental collaboration through features such as a shared daily log and an emergency information feature that provides important care details when needed.

Weaknesses:
User fatigue and burnout could make consistent daily data entry difficult. Managing multiple types of medical and care-related information would also require an extremely simple and carefully designed interface to avoid adding additional stress for users.

Reason for Rejection:
Similar health and caregiver tracking solutions already provide many of the proposed features. The team determined that the concept would require stronger differentiation and a clearer unique value proposition to stand out from existing solutions.

Idea 2: Used Games Exchange Platform

Strengths:
The platform would provide gamers with a dedicated place to buy, sell, and exchange used games and gaming accessories. It could help users access lower-cost second-hand products, find specific games, connect with other gamers, and encourage the reuse of gaming products.

Weaknesses:
The platform would require a large and active user base to maintain enough listings and offers. Trust and safety could also become an issue when users purchase used products from individuals. Additional challenges include verifying product condition and compatibility and competing with established marketplaces.

Reason for Rejection:
The concept is already available through established platforms such as Haraj and other classified marketplaces, where users can buy and sell used games, consoles, and accessories. Therefore, the idea did not provide enough differentiation or a strong unique value proposition compared with existing platforms.

4. Evaluation and Selection of Ideas
Evaluation Criteria

The team evaluated the proposed concepts using the following criteria:

Problem Relevance: Whether the idea addresses a clear and meaningful real-world problem.
Technical Feasibility: Whether the team can implement the required technical components using its current skills and available resources.
MVP Feasibility: Whether the core functionality can be completed within the project timeframe.
Team Skills Alignment: Whether the project allows the team to apply and develop its skills in frontend development, backend development, UI/UX, APIs, and GIS integration.
Innovation and Differentiation: Whether the solution provides meaningful value compared with existing alternatives.
Scalability: Whether the solution can support additional features and future development beyond the initial MVP.
Selection Process

The team compared the proposed ideas using these criteria and discussed their strengths, limitations, technical requirements, and existing alternatives.

The first two concepts addressed identifiable user needs but presented challenges related to market differentiation and competition with existing solutions. MAMAR provided a clearer opportunity to combine the team’s software development skills with location-based functionality while addressing a specific coordination problem.

Based on this evaluation, the team selected MAMAR as the project concept to develop further.

5. Selected MVP Concept
Selected Idea Summary

MAMAR is a GIS-enabled B2B platform that connects contractors with traffic control service providers to manage temporary traffic control operations for construction and roadwork projects. Contractors can define their work zones on an interactive map, request traffic control services, and coordinate the implementation of temporary traffic elements such as road diversions, barriers, and traffic signs. Service providers can manage requests and document field implementation using geographic locations and site information.

The Problem It Solves

Construction and roadwork projects often require temporary traffic control measures, such as road diversions, barriers, traffic signs, and temporary traffic routes around work zones. Coordinating these services between contractors and traffic control service providers can involve multiple parties and requires clear location-based information.

MAMAR addresses this problem by providing a centralized platform where contractors can define their work zones, request the required traffic control services, connect with suitable service providers, and document implementation geographically.

Target Audience
Contractors: Construction, infrastructure, and roadwork contractors who require temporary traffic control services around their project work zones.
Traffic Control Service Providers: Companies that provide and implement temporary traffic control services, such as road diversions, barriers, traffic signs, and temporary traffic routes.
Key Features

1. Interactive Work Zone Map:
Contractors can mark the project area and visualize the work zone on an interactive map.

2. Service Request & Provider Selection:
Contractors can specify the required traffic control services, submit service requests, and select a service provider for the project.

3. Project Status & Field Documentation:
Both parties can follow the request and project status, while service providers can document completed field work and update relevant project information.

Rationale & Alignment

Feasibility:
The project is achievable within the available timeframe using the team’s technical skills in frontend and backend development, UI/UX, API integration, and GIS. The MVP has a defined scope that allows the team to develop and integrate the core system components progressively.

Innovation & Impact:
MAMAR brings contractors and traffic control service providers into one digital workflow, simplifying service coordination and improving visibility throughout the process. The platform combines service management with location-based capabilities to provide a more organized approach to temporary traffic control operations.

Scalability:
The system is designed to support future growth by allowing new services and features to be added without changing the core workflow. Future versions could include real-time tracking, automated notifications, payment integration, advanced project dashboards, and additional traffic management services.

Potential Challenges & Opportunities

Challenges:
Managing development time effectively across frontend, backend, UI/UX, system integration, and the preparation of the required GIS layers and spatial data may be challenging. The team will manage this by prioritizing MVP functionality, distributing responsibilities clearly, and reviewing progress regularly.

Opportunities:
MAMAR has the potential to evolve beyond the initial MVP into a broader solution for temporary traffic control and work-zone management. Its location-based approach could create future opportunities for adoption by contractors and traffic control service providers, integration with relevant external systems, and expansion across infrastructure projects in Saudi Arabia.
